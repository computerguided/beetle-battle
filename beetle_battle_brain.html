Beetle Battle, taught by itself

The original game shipped with a hand-tuned heuristic. Here the computer is given no opening knowledge at all — it plays against itself, learns a value function from its own wins and losses, and then you sit down across the board from whatever it has become.

Let the machine practice

Choose how many games it may play against itself. Every move of every game nudges ten weights — material, territory, corners, exposure, chain potential — toward whatever actually wins.

Step 2 · The match

Now try to beat it

Place a beetle on an empty square or one you already own. Fill a square to its capacity — the number of neighbours — and the beetles explode outward, converting everything they land on. Eliminate every enemy beetle to win.

Press “New game” when the brain is ready.

An untrained machine plays at random. Train it above, then come back.

Method. Afterstate TD(0) self-play. The value of a position is σ(w·f) — a sigmoid over ten normalised board features — read as the mover’s probability of winning. After each of its own moves the agent updates its previous prediction toward the new one (ε-greedy exploration, decaying), and toward 1 / 0 at a win / loss. Play uses the same one-ply greedy search the original engine used, only with learned weights instead of hand-tuned ones.

A learning-lab remake of computerguided/beetle-battle by Fred Dijkstra — same rules, same red and blue beetles, new brain.
